# Abuja neighbourhood — planning brief

## Scope and honesty

Vertualworld is being re-themed as a **small, schematic neighbourhood in Abuja, Federal Capital Territory, Nigeria**. It is not a street-for-street reproduction of a named Abuja district, and its compressed transport network should not be read as a real route map. District-specific labels and claims of geographic accuracy are intentionally avoided until a real district is chosen.

## Research basis

- The FCTA describes Abuja through planned phases, districts, sector centres and detailed site-development plans. Its engineering department identifies the original Phase I districts as including Garki, Wuse, Maitama, Asokoro and Central Area. The game should therefore read as a neighbourhood within a larger planned city, not as a self-contained island-town.
- Abuja’s neighbourhood-unit idea puts everyday services close to homes: the planning account by Nigerian town planner Tpl. Umar Shuaibu lists schools, a family clinic, shopping, a community hall and other local services, with higher-order needs graduating to the district and city centre. It also says basic neighbourhood needs should not require residents to cross major arterials.
- FCTA’s transportation remit covers public transport as well as pedestrian and cycle infrastructure. In-game rail and bus should be connected to walkable public space, rather than treated as isolated attractions.
- FCTA describes Millennium Park’s formal, rectilinear path, fountains and contrasting landscape zones. This is a reference for a clear civic green spine and layered public realm—not a claim that the game reproduces Millennium Park.
- Abuja heat-island research associates hotter surfaces with bare and built-up land and recommends more trees and higher-albedo surfaces. The public realm should therefore use shade, planted areas and lighter paving, while avoiding unnecessary hardscape.
- Abuja’s City Gate, Aso Rock and civic parks are useful identity references. The game may use a stylised landmark/view, but should not imply the tiny playable town is the whole capital or an exact landmark site.

## Translation into the playable plan

1. **Inland setting:** remove the ocean-and-cliff composition. Retain gentle terrain and a warm savannah edge; use a distant Aso Rock-inspired silhouette as a landscape reference rather than a literal survey model.
2. **Urban hierarchy:** treat the existing homes as a local residential neighbourhood; keep the café and market as everyday services; add a modest Unity Community Hall and forecourt near the service cluster; retain the civic plaza, stadium, and three linked transit hubs as higher-order destinations. This is a planning hierarchy for play, not a claim about real district boundaries.
3. **Walkability and shade:** keep the existing pedestrian path and connected street grid; make the civic route legible, give the hall a shaded meeting forecourt, and protect planting/open space. The small scale is not a reason to route every local trip across a fast arterial.
4. **Transit:** preserve the existing bus and rail services, all three stops/terminals, and their movement behavior. A route review found shallow bus/rail approaches near project coordinates [44, 8] and [47, 33], plus a local-road approach only about 0.66 world units from the rail near [44, 40]. These remain known design risks, not verified safe crossings; route geometry is unchanged in this theme pass. Any future revision should validate clearance, approach angles, and crossing controls rather than relying on coordinate proximity alone.
5. **Local identity without caricature:** use Abuja/FCT naming and a restrained palette of savannah greens, warm earth, pale civic paving and granite tones. Avoid falsely assigning a district or using generic “African” motifs as a substitute for context.
6. **Economy and safety:** retain NPC rentals, cafés/market goods and fictional in-game credits. The phone bank remains a local game ledger/demo; no real account details, money movement or payment settlement are introduced.
