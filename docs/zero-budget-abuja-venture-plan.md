# Vertualworld: a zero-cash start in Abuja, with a global ambition

**Strategy brief · 7 October 2026**

## Executive recommendation

Treat “trillions of naira monthly” as a **north-star ambition, not a forecast or promise**. The credible route is not to build the biggest 3D city. It is to turn Vertualworld into a mobile-first platform that makes local places, events and stories interactive; helps organisers and merchants attract and measure participation; and eventually gives local creators tools to publish reusable experiences in many cities.

**First revenue wedge to test:** an interactive activation for an Abuja event, venue or sponsor. Keep the player experience free and short. The organiser may control the audience and execution, while a brand or agency may fund sponsor engagement; **which party actually pays is an open question**, not a settled customer choice. Interview both before choosing a payer. Universities, cultural organisations, tourism bodies and everyday merchants are adjacent prospects, not four extra products to build on day one. Avoid making public-sector procurement the first revenue dependency. This tests whether the existing 3D neighbourhood can support a measurable campaign before spending on multiplayer servers, a creator marketplace, payment infrastructure or paid advertising.

Abuja is the proving ground, **not an assumed district map**. Keep the in-game plan schematic until a real-world pilot location and source data are agreed with partners. Use real venue details only with permission and label virtual distances honestly. Current competitor research also found browser-based Abuja life-world products and a fast-growing Nigerian social simulator with an announced Abuja expansion; the detailed comparison is in the [competitor landscape report](competitor-landscape-abuja.md).

## What the ambition means in money

A trillion-naira monthly target is exceptionally large:

- **₦1 trillion monthly revenue = ₦12 trillion annual revenue.** Revenue is not profit, company valuation, transaction volume or the founder’s personal income.
- At a **10% marketplace take rate**, it would take **₦10 trillion of monthly transactions** to book ₦1 trillion of revenue before costs.
- At **₦10,000 monthly revenue per paying account**, it would take **100 million paying accounts**; at ₦1,000, it would take one billion.

These are arithmetic illustrations, not projections. Abuja alone is not a plausible ceiling for that ambition. A company at that scale would need repeatable global products, enormous customer and partner networks, strong retention, reliable infrastructure, high trust, and years of execution. First prove one repeatable sale.

## What exists already—and what does not

Vertualworld already has a useful demonstration asset: a lightweight browser-based 3D neighbourhood with homes and NPC rentals, cafés and a market, fictional `GC` credits, roads, bus and rail, a stadium, a phone interface and a field map. The Abuja re-theme has added an inland landscape, an illustrative Aso Rock-inspired horizon, Unity Court service labels and a small community hall.

The current app is still a **single-user demo**, not an online platform: there is no shared world, production account system, creator dashboard, partner analytics service, moderation operation or real payment settlement. Local browser saves are not a scalable customer database. Do not pitch those missing capabilities as if they exist.

The strategic move is to convert the demo into a *measurable service* for a narrowly defined first buyer, then add technology only when customer evidence justifies it.

## Research findings and how much confidence to place in them

1. **Abuja has a planned hierarchy.** FCTA material on [urban planning](https://www.fcta.gov.ng/ova_dep/urban-and-regional-planning/) describes phases, districts, sector centres and detailed development plans. Nigerian town planner Tpl. Umar Shuaibu’s [account of the Abuja neighbourhood concept](https://dailytrust.com/abuja-masterplan-the-indigenous-input-ii/) describes daily needs such as shopping and a community hall at neighbourhood scale, with higher-order needs graduating to district and city scale. That supports a neighbourhood-first design, but it does not make Vertualworld’s map geographically accurate.
2. **Mobile distribution matters.** The Nigerian Communications Commission’s [2026–2030 spectrum roadmap](https://ncc.gov.ng/media/5489/view) reports 144.8 million active internet subscriptions in November 2025 and gives an average of about 8.5 GB of monthly data per internet subscription. These are subscriptions, **not unique people or a count of Vertualworld customers**. The useful product implication is to test on ordinary phones, make data and loading time visible, and retain a 2D fallback if 3D makes the experience too heavy.
3. **Gaming-market headline numbers need skepticism.** A [Games Industry Africa article](https://gamesindustryafrica.com/2025/11/05/nigerias-gaming-market-surpasses-46-million-players-as-esports-gains-national-momentum/) about Maliyo Games’ 2025 report says 46.6 million active players in one passage but lists 6.6 million in its key-insights bullets. Because those figures conflict, this plan does **not** use either as a reliable Nigerian market-size estimate. Obtain and reconcile the underlying report, then validate the Abuja audience directly.
4. **Climate-conscious public space is relevant.** A 2025 [Abuja urban-heat study](https://publications.umyu.edu.ng/scientifica/index.php/usci/article/view/129) reports hotter bare and built-up surfaces and recommends tree planting and higher-albedo surfaces. That supports shade, greenery and pale surfaces as design cues; it is not a claim that a tiny game scene can change city temperatures.
5. **Payments and personal data are regulated, not launch shortcuts.** The [Nigeria Data Protection Act](https://ndpc.gov.ng/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Nigeria_Data_Protection_Act_2023.pdf) covers personal-data processing, including transparency, security, children and data-subject rights. The CBN’s [mobile-money framework](https://www.cbn.gov.ng/Out/2021/CCD/Framework%20and%20Guidelines%20on%20Mobile%20Money%20Services%20in%20Nigeria%20-%20July%202021.pdf) describes regulated models for mobile money. Flutterwave’s current [security guidance](https://developer.flutterwave.com/docs/best-practices) says secret-key API calls belong on a backend, and payment events should be verified before value is delivered. Get current Nigerian legal/accounting advice before offering paid products; this brief is not legal advice.

## Unknowns this plan does not pretend to have solved

There is not yet evidence that Abuja event organisers or sponsors will pay for a 3D campaign, that players will return, that an on-site QR action changes attendance or sales, or that the 3D experience beats a simple mobile webpage. New Abuja-set browser life worlds and Lagos Life’s reported Abuja expansion also compete for the same local attention; their existence does not establish audience share or durable retention. There is no validated payer, price, customer acquisition cost, retention cohort or support cost. Treat every product and revenue claim below as a testable hypothesis until a real buyer pays, a player cohort returns and delivery costs are measured.

## The first product: a sponsored Abuja playable campaign

**Working offer:** “Turn one Abuja event or venue into a short browser quest that people can open from a link or QR code, play on a phone, and complete with an optional real-world check-in. The organiser gets an aggregate results summary.”

A first campaign could be a three-minute, three-step “field quest” in the current Unity Court world, with a clearly labelled partner destination. A player might discover a story, visit a participating stall or event desk, and scan a partner-issued QR code to receive a **merchant-controlled discount or event acknowledgement**. For ticketed events, link to the organiser’s current ticket page and leave payment, attendee check-in and ticket records with that provider. The app should not need continuous GPS, card details, a cash balance or an account just to play.

**Player value:** a free, quick, locally authored experience that works on a phone and is worth sharing.

**Buyer value:** a branded activation, event engagement and a way to count completed quests or voluntary QR redemptions. Promise only what the pilot actually measures; do not promise footfall, sales or “viral” reach before you have evidence.

**First buyers and partners to interview:**

- Event and festival organisers who need an interactive activity or sponsor deliverable and can provide audience access.
- Sponsors, brands and agencies that may pay for an activation; test them separately from the organiser who supplies the event and distribution.
- Venues, cultural organisations, universities and community organisations that host repeat events.
- Cafés, markets and retailers that can offer a small discount and want measurable visits.

Do not begin by trying to sell individual players virtual currency. Keep access free during validation. The earliest payer should be an organisation with a budget and a specific campaign outcome, but the discovery work must establish who controls that budget.

## Abuja-first go-to-market, without pretending it is a district map

Start with one **real partner cluster selected by interviews**, not a guessed district. The first campaign can still run inside the schematic game neighbourhood; its real-world venue and any on-site QR instructions must be accurate and separately labelled. Use Abuja relationships and local creators to decide what stories are welcome. Do not claim FCTA, university, community or brand partnership until there is a written agreement.

### First 14 days: customer discovery

Do not add a major feature yet. Speak to at least:

- 10 people who play mobile games or attend local events;
- 6 venue, merchant or event operators;
- 3 event, campus, cultural or tourism organisers;
- 3–5 sponsor, brand or agency budget owners; and
- 1–2 local artists, writers or game creators.

Ask about recent behaviour, not hypothetical enthusiasm:

- How did the organiser attract people to the last event, and which tools already handled registration, tickets, check-in and reminders?
- Who controlled the activation budget, who supplied the audience, what was spent, and how were attendance or conversions measured?
- What would have to be true for a sponsor or organiser to pay for a short playable activation instead of a normal post, listing or billboard?
- What phone, network and data constraints did testers actually encounter, and what do they currently use instead of a 3D world?
- Would a player finish a three-minute quest, share it, or use a partner link/QR code—and what result would the buyer count as valuable?

Show the working preview on the person’s own phone where possible. Ask for a **specific next step**—an introduction to the budget owner, permission to run a small test, a written expression of interest or a paid pilot proposal—not just “I like it.” Keep interview notes anonymised unless a person explicitly agrees to be contacted.

### Days 15–30: one focused prototype

Build only what the strongest buyer problem requires: one short quest, a partner-approved identity, a shareable link/QR landing path, a merchant-issued offer if appropriate, and an aggregate results sheet. Do not make a self-service editor or login system unless several qualified buyers need it.

Recruit a small, voluntary phone-test group. Record only the minimum needed to learn: quest opened, first step completed, quest finished, optional QR redemption, device class, load failure. Prefer aggregate counts or short-lived pseudonymous identifiers; do not collect precise movement histories by default.

### Days 31–90: prove payment and repeatability

Pitch a fixed-scope pilot with a written deliverable and success measure. Set the test price from the delivery hours and direct costs, then compare it with the buyer’s current event-promotion spend and stated budget; do not invent a market rate from a spreadsheet. If no one will pay yet, one tightly bounded in-kind or free test can be worthwhile **only** if it provides access to the audience, permission to publish a case study, and real outcome data. Do not do unlimited unpaid custom work.

Before accepting money, confirm the proper business, tax, contract and payment arrangements with qualified local advisers. Do not accept a sponsor’s money into the in-game wallet or hold customer funds on behalf of a merchant. If paid delivery is not legally or operationally ready, obtain a non-binding expression of interest or run a clearly free test while setting up correctly.

**Day-90 decision gate:** continue this wedge only if a real buyer—organiser, venue, sponsor or agency—has paid for a scoped pilot or committed credibly to a repeat, with the event/venue partner also agreeing to audience access, and the player experience is usable on target phones. If players enjoy it but no organisation pays, test a different buyer or a simpler event-service offer before adding more game features.

## Start from ₦0: what that can and cannot mean

A zero-cash start is possible because the game, code and public demo already exist. It does **not** mean the venture has zero cost: time, electricity, phone access, mobile data, transport, production labour and partner support all have value.

Use the assets already available:

- The current browser build and existing public hosting; do not rent servers for a one-player prototype.
- Phone screen recording, a short demo link, a one-page offer and a QR code generated with free tools.
- Direct introductions, community groups and partner-owned channels instead of paid advertising.
- Manually configured campaigns and a spreadsheet of aggregate counts instead of a custom admin system.
- Existing Three.js assets and open-source tools; keep initial content small and reuseable.

Keep an honest time ledger even if the founder is not drawing a salary. A business that looks profitable only because design, sales, moderation and support are treated as free will not scale sustainably. Avoid asking collaborators for open-ended unpaid work; agree credit, rights, scope and any future revenue share in writing.

**First use of earned cash:** formalise the business and bookkeeping as needed; protect creator rights and customer data; buy only the domain, hosting, legal/accounting advice or devices that remove a proven delivery bottleneck. Do not spend the first revenue on a bigger map, expensive ads or speculative token systems.

## Revenue model, in the right order

| Stage | Who pays | What they pay for | Readiness condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Abuja campaign service | Event organiser, venue, sponsor or institution | Fixed-scope branded quest, event activity and results summary | One repeatable campaign and evidence the buyer values the result |
| 2. Campaign subscription | Repeat venues/organisers | A monthly publishing slot, campaign templates and aggregate reporting | Several repeat buyers and a supportable workflow |
| 3. Creator services | Brands, venues and creators | Commissioned stories, environments, asset packs or campaign production | Clear licences, rights, quality review and paid creator terms |
| 4. Creator/partner marketplace | Buyers and creators | Commission on optional bookings, digital work or services | Real demand, dispute handling, refunds, moderation and a lawful payment design |
| 5. Multi-city licensing and APIs | Universities, event groups, tourism bodies, retailers, agencies and platform partners | White-label experiences, reusable tools, software access and integrations | Proven implementation in more than one city without rebuilding everything by hand |
| 6. Consumer digital extras | Players | Optional cosmetics, memberships or expansions | Strong retention, platform-policy review, transparent pricing and consumer safeguards |

A transaction commission is **revenue only on the take rate**, not on the full amount paid through the system. If users buy a merchant’s meal, the meal is not Vertualworld revenue. Do not introduce paid chance rewards, betting-like mechanics, cash-out game credits, crypto tokens or “play-to-earn” promises as a shortcut.

## Competition—and a defensible difference

The 7 October 2026 competitor scan found direct Abuja-set browser life worlds, including [AbjLife.app](https://abjlife.app/) and [AbujaLife at abujacity.life](https://abujacity.life/), plus [Lagos Life](https://lagoslife.app/) with an announced Abuja/Port Harcourt expansion. Their public claims overlap with city exploration, transport, homes, jobs, player interaction and—in some cases—advertising. Pages about live versus upcoming features are inconsistent, and public audience/revenue claims are not independently audited. **A browser-based 3D Abuja setting alone is not differentiated.**

| Alternative | What it already does well | How Vertualworld should respond |
|---|---|---|
| Abuja Life products and Lagos Life | Local 3D city-life worlds, social play, mapped or locally authored places, virtual economies and, in some cases, advertiser inventory | Do not race on map fidelity, social scale or billboards. Keep the existing scene as a demo; test a partner-paid activation and a measurable action. |
| Roblox, Fortnite/UEFN and ZEPETO | Large creator/social ecosystems, cross-device discovery, creator monetisation and brand worlds/ads | Do not try to build their audience, creator economy or brand tooling. Win only on a specific local buyer workflow that those platforms are not solving. |
| Maliyo Games and Abuja creators | Locally grounded game craft, cultural storytelling, missions, rewards and event activations | Treat local identity as essential but not exclusive. Explore collaboration; differentiate through repeatable campaign delivery and clear results. |
| Tix Africa and other event platforms | Event pages, ticket sales, reminders, discovery, check-in and event analytics; Tix lists 8% + ₦100 per paid Nigerian ticket | Link to the organiser’s existing ticket page. Do not take payments, own attendee records or duplicate check-in. |
| Google Business Profile and WhatsApp Business | Free/familiar local discovery, reviews, catalogues, links, QR entry points and customer conversations | Do not sell a basic listing. Prove that an interactive story or quest adds measurable engagement and routes into existing booking/order tools. |
| Selar and creator-commerce tools | Existing sales pages, payments and tools for creators’ products, services and tickets | Link to creators’ established stores; defer a Vertualworld marketplace and payouts. |
| Spatial’s former creator platform | A cautionary example: Spatial ended free/pro 3D-world hosting in July 2026 while retaining Enterprise | Keep hosting and moderation costs low, preserve asset portability and add multiplayer only after a funded use case. |

The near-term positioning hypothesis is **an Abuja-grounded interactive campaign layer**: a short, partner-approved playable quest that starts from existing channels, sends people to the organiser’s ticket/booking/WhatsApp destination and measures only its own verified steps. The first payer is unproven; test whether an organiser/venue or a sponsor/agency funds it. This campaign service and its analytics do not exist in the current app yet. The fuller evidence, claims and caveats are in the [competitor landscape report](competitor-landscape-abuja.md). Any defensible advantage—local creator relationships, repeatable setup and trusted outcome reporting—must be earned through repeat buyers and measured results, not assumed from the Abuja setting.

## Product and technology roadmap

### Now: static, single-player proof

Keep the game as a fast browser demo. Fix critical mobile issues, create one campaign by hand and measure the smallest useful funnel. Keep fictional `GC` closed-loop, non-transferable, non-redeemable and separate from naira.

### After a paying pilot: a small campaign service

Add only the backend needed to publish partner-approved campaign content and return aggregate results. Establish authentication and access controls for organisers, consent and retention rules, backups, logging, rate limits and a way to delete campaign data. Separate the content configuration from the renderer so a campaign can be reused without copying the whole world.

### After repeat campaigns: creator tools and multi-city support

Build a controlled creator workflow: templates, accessibility checks, asset rights, moderation and review before publication. Store reusable world components separately from local data. Add new cities only with local partners and sourced place information; the Abuja scene must not be relabelled as a true map of another city.

### Only after proven concurrent use: online scale

A performant Three.js frame on one phone is not proof of internet-scale infrastructure. Shared users, accounts, live events, asset delivery, moderation, analytics, anti-abuse, customer support and payment events require separate load testing and operational budgets. Move from a static site to managed, observable services in stages; test expected concurrency, p95 latency, failure recovery, bandwidth cost and moderation load before a public launch. Do not build a large always-on multiplayer backend before users need it.

## Trust, regulation and payments

- **Privacy by design:** comply with the Nigeria Data Protection Act; state the purpose, collect the minimum, set a retention period, secure data and give people a way to exercise their rights. Complete a privacy and child-safety review before targeting minors or introducing accounts, chat, location history or profiling. QR-based voluntary check-ins are preferable to continuous GPS for the first pilot.
- **Children and community safety:** keep open chat and unmoderated uploads off the first release. Provide reporting and removal procedures, clear community rules, safe sponsor content and parental/age safeguards if children use the service. Do not use a children’s audience as an advertising-data shortcut.
- **Money:** today’s Abuja Game Wallet is a demo ledger, not a bank account or a real payment product. Keep GC fictional. If the product later accepts real payment, use a properly contracted licensed payment partner and a secure server-side integration; keep secret keys out of browser code; verify webhook signatures and transaction status, amount, currency and reference before delivering anything; make refunds and disputes explicit. Do not hold or issue stored value without specialist regulatory advice and any required authorisation.
- **Commercial integrity:** identify sponsored quests as sponsored, obtain licences for music, photographs, locations, names and creator work, and agree who handles a promotion or discount. Get advice on Nigerian company, tax, consumer-protection, advertising, gaming and payments obligations before a commercial launch.

## Metrics that determine whether to scale

Measure outcomes rather than downloads or time spent alone.

**For players:** first-interaction load time on a named baseline Android phone/network; quest starts and completions; return rate at Day 1, Day 7 and Day 30; shares/invites; crashes and data use. Compare 3D with a lighter 2D view. These targets must be set from real cohorts and are not universal benchmarks.

**For partners:** qualified QR redemptions, cost per verified action, campaign delivery hours, buyer satisfaction, renewal rate and the partner’s own comparison with its current channels. A scan is not automatically a sale; report those separately.

**For the business:** paid-pilot conversion, repeat revenue, gross margin after properly priced labour and support, cash collection time, refund/dispute rate and months of runway. Do not scale paid acquisition until retention and partner renewal are repeatable.

A simple pilot model is:

> **Campaign contribution = fee received − creator/design time − support/moderation − delivery and hosting − payment fees/refunds − tax and other direct costs.**

If contribution is negative after counting founder labour, redesign the offer or price before increasing volume.

## A practical first-week checklist

1. Record a 30–60 second phone walkthrough of the current Abuja preview. Show the mission, map and local services; do not claim real-world route accuracy.
2. Draft a one-page pilot offer: one 3-minute quest, one partner, one event window, one optional QR action, one aggregate report, explicit limits and a case-study permission request.
3. Make a list of 20 Abuja people to interview through real introductions; ask who owns the event/marketing budget.
4. Test the demo on several ordinary Android phones and mobile connections. Note load time, failed frames, data use and where people stop.
5. Ask three organisations for a bounded pilot conversation. Seek a real next step or budget signal, not compliments.
6. Do not add live payments, sign-in, open chat, real-money GC, precise GPS, crypto or a large multiplayer server during this discovery week.
7. At the end of the week, review evidence and choose one customer problem. If no one has a painful, funded problem, continue discovery instead of coding more scenery.

## The long-term institution-scale path

A globally important Vertualworld would need to become more than an Abuja game: a trusted publishing and engagement platform for interactive local places, creator-owned stories and measurable campaigns across multiple markets. Its defensibility would come from software that is genuinely reusable, a network of skilled creators and paying partners, locally governed content, strong safety and privacy, and distribution that works on ordinary phones. A city-by-city service agency can earn money, but it will not reach global scale unless delivery becomes repeatable software and creator infrastructure.

The claim to global relevance should eventually be earned through exports, well-paid creative and technical jobs, intellectual property, reliable public-interest partnerships and proven customer outcomes—not through hype or a “metaverse” label. Abuja is a strong place to start learning; no political access, trillion-naira revenue or investor interest is guaranteed.
