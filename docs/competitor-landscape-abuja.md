# Vertualworld competitor landscape: Abuja-first

**Research snapshot · 7 October 2026**

**Scope:** direct 3D/social-world competitors and substitutes for attention, local promotion, event operations and creator monetisation. Abuja is the intended first market. Findings are a dated snapshot; live product pages, player counters and prices can change quickly.

## Executive summary

The competitive picture is materially closer than “Roblox and Fortnite are big.” There are now public browser-based life-simulation products explicitly set in Abuja, alongside Lagos Life’s fast-moving Nigerian social-world launch and announced Abuja/Port Harcourt expansion. These products overlap with Vertualworld’s current *city-world* framing: 3D places, local landmarks, avatars, homes, transport, jobs, player interaction and, in some cases, business advertising.

That means **“a 3D Abuja neighbourhood in a browser” is not a defensible position by itself**. A zero-budget Vertualworld should not try to win a map-accuracy or persistent-MMO race. The more testable near-term position is a **small, browser-accessible interactive campaign layer**: a locally authored quest or story that an Abuja venue, event organiser or sponsor can distribute through channels it already uses, and that sends people to its existing ticket, booking, WhatsApp or sales flow. The product does not yet have the campaign-management or outcome-reporting service to sell this; those are hypotheses to validate, not current capabilities.

The first payer is not settled. Treat **organiser/venue versus sponsor/brand/agency** as a customer-discovery test: the organiser may control the audience and event operations while a sponsor may own the activation budget. Tix Africa already handles much of ticketing and event execution; Google Business Profile and WhatsApp Business offer strong low-cost merchant alternatives. Vertualworld must demonstrate incremental, attributable engagement rather than claim to replace them.

**Important evidence limits:** the newly found Abuja products’ public pages contain inconsistent statements about what is live versus “coming next,” and no independently verified audience or revenue figures were found for them. Lagos Life’s live counters, advertising rates and early revenue claims are operator-published and change rapidly; they are not audited unique users, retention, profit or sales lift. No competitor’s advertised price is a reliable Vertualworld price benchmark.

## 1. Direct Abuja and Nigerian world competitors

### AbjLife.app — real-map Abuja exploration plus life-simulation systems

The [AbjLife.app homepage](https://abjlife.app/) markets a free 3D Abuja game that runs in a mobile or desktop browser without a download. It claims roads and buildings from Abuja’s real map, photo-based landmarks, real-road distances and taxi fares, a day/night cycle, and culturally specific avatar options and city details. Its landing page lists places including City Gate, the National Mosque, Christian Centre, Eagle Square, Millennium Park, IbroFish and Magic Land.

The public [play route](https://abjlife.app/play), checked on 7 October, goes beyond the homepage pitch: it displays a social city HUD, chat/messages, player-owned businesses, jobs paid from business tills, rankings, elections and a phone-based ad interface. It says businesses can advertise “to every player” from **₦500 a week** and that candidates can place posters on billboards, taxis and streets. The [account-creation page](https://abjlife.app/play/create) asks players to claim a username and create an account. The site also says there is no crypto coin/token and that future top-ups would be for in-game money.

**Evidence caveat:** the homepage still describes other players, jobs, homes/property and additional districts as “coming next,” while `/play` displays social, jobs, business, politics and advertising systems. The public pages do not clarify whether the ₦500 advertising amount is real-money inventory, game currency or simply a displayed starting price, and they disclose no independently verified audience, retention, ad delivery or revenue. The product’s maker/organisation and its relationship, if any, to other Abuja Life-branded pages were not established from the pages reviewed. Treat the functionality and pricing as public product claims to verify in a direct demo—not as audited operations.

**What it means:** this is direct competition for an “explore Abuja in a 3D browser world” story and potentially for local business-ad budgets. Vertualworld’s schematic neighbourhood should not be positioned as geographically accurate or as the only Abuja virtual world. Competing on thousands of mapped buildings, transport fares, player-run businesses or city-scale social systems would be a major, expensive scope change.

### AbujaLife at abujacity.life — account-based 3D life world

The public [AbujaLife page](https://abujacity.life/) renders an interactive 3D starter home and neighbourhood in a browser. It invites a resident to create an account, move around a Garki starter studio, arrange a home and build a persistent life; it explicitly says the displayed NGN/naira balance is game money. The page showed a playable-looking scene and account form when checked on 7 October, but did not disclose public player counts, commercial rates or a complete feature roadmap. I could not verify whether this product is associated with AbjLife.app, Lagos Life or any public code repository using a similar name.

**What it means:** even if early, another Abuja life-world can occupy the same simple category description. The opportunity is not to win by saying “ours is also 3D and Abuja-themed.” The buyer, use case, partner workflow, distinctive content and measured result need to be recognisably different.

### Lagos Life — social life simulator, direct advertising and multi-city expansion

[Lagos Life](https://lagoslife.app/) launched on 1 October 2026 as a browser-based social life simulation. Its official pages describe players creating characters, living in a shared city, working, managing a virtual economy and interacting with other players. The creator, Shalom Rayhamen, posted on 6 October that **Abuja and Port Harcourt were live**; the game’s Terms also describe a single world with multiple locations. I did not independently test the Abuja/Port Harcourt play areas, so treat their exact status and depth as a rapidly changing launch claim, not a completed product audit.

The [official live-statistics page](https://lagoslife.app/stats) displayed **2,782,721 total players, 95,726 online and 25,754,537 all-time visits** when checked on 7 October. These are live, operator-published dashboard counters, not an independent audit; “players,” visits, unique people and retained users are not interchangeable. On 5 October, [Techpoint Africa reported](https://techpoint.africa/news/lagos-life-1m-players/) the dashboard at 1,212,477 total players and 71,625 online. It said the counters were not independently verified and relayed user questions about the counting method, including one user’s claim that sign-ups did not require email verification. The creator separately reported **US$46,900 revenue in the first four days** on [her X account](https://x.com/Shalom_HeyEliy/status/2107196383379808484); it is a self-reported gross-revenue claim, not audited profit or a forecast for another game.

Lagos Life monetises directly as well as through its virtual economy. Its [advertising page](https://lagoslife.app/advertise), checked on 7 October, listed 38 digital road billboards, rotating ads, lagoon “sea plots,” stats-page placements and airport placements; it displayed **₦250,000 per billboard for seven days**, ₦18,000 per sea plot for seven days, and ₦150,000 for a stats-page ad for seven days. By contrast, Techpoint’s 5 October article reported that the same public rate card then showed ₦5,000 per billboard per week. The page says ads rotate and placements are assigned to less crowded boards. Prices and packaging therefore appear volatile or to have changed; treat both as dated observations, **not** an established market rate or proof that inventory sold. The creator also reported one campaign’s taps relative to app opens; that is not an independently audited impression-to-sale conversion.

The Terms of Use set an intended audience of **18+**, distinguish fictional “Game Naira” from legal tender, contemplate real-money purchases of in-game units and advertising, and say advertising does not guarantee impressions, sales or other results. These are the operator’s published terms, not an independent legal/compliance assessment.

**What it means:** Lagos Life is the clearest signal that local context, a link-to-browser entry path and social play can generate attention quickly—and that a concentrated audience can attract direct advertiser interest. It is also the strongest warning against taking a viral launch as proof of durable retention or profits. Vertualworld should compete on a tightly scoped partner outcome, not on self-reported player totals or undifferentiated virtual billboards. If the local campaign route proves real buyer value, assess whether a small activation can be hosted in Vertualworld or distributed through partners; do not assume a standalone world is required.

### Related fast-moving browser games

Techpoint’s report also describes [Lagos Run](https://techpoint.africa/feature/adediran-opeyemi/), a small browser game built around driving a danfo through Lagos, and fast follow-on projects including PH Lifestyle and Road to Number One. The lesson is not to clone these games: a simple, culturally specific loop with no app-store download can spread quickly, and successful concepts can be copied within days. Local flavour alone is not a moat; return reasons, community trust, creator rights, distribution and repeatable partner outcomes matter.

## 2. Global platforms and branded-world playbooks

### Roblox — creator platform, discovery, advertising and browser reach

Roblox’s official investor overview reports **123 million daily active users and 29 billion hours engaged in Q2 2026**. At its September 2026 Developers Conference, Roblox announced plans for creators to make experiences available as standalone apps, let players launch experiences in a browser without installing the app, add prompt-based game creation, and offer eligible US-based creators a real-currency Wallet with business-day payouts. Roblox said creators earned about **US$1.7 billion in the prior 12 months**. The Wallet rollout was initially for eligible US creators aged 18+, with broader international expansion described as a future step. Roblox’s creator documentation lists in-game purchases, subscriptions, avatar goods, Creator Rewards, ads and physical-commerce integrations.

**Threat:** an immense player/discovery and creator economy that Vertualworld cannot match at zero budget; a benchmark for browser convenience, creator tooling and brand monetisation. **Response:** do not sell generic UGC or “a metaverse.” Use existing social and event channels to bring a small, local audience to a distinct campaign and link them back to established services.

### Fortnite / UEFN — engagement-funded islands and paid promotion

Epic’s [Engagement Payout documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/fortnite/engagement-payout-in-fortnite-creative?lang=en-US) says 40% of eligible net revenue from Fortnite’s Item Shop and related purchases enters a creator payout pool. Allocation considers active playtime, retention and bringing new or lapsed players. Epic’s [Sponsored Row documentation](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/fortnite/campaign-faqs-in-fortnite) describes clearly labelled paid placements bought through a bid/budget campaign system with impression-based reporting and moderation; 100% of Sponsored Row revenue goes into the pool through the end of 2026, with 50% described as the long-term rate. Epic also permits approved [brand islands](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fortnite/converting-your-island-into-a-brand-island-in-fortnite), subject to developer-program eligibility, licensing and brand-rule review.

**Threat:** professional brand-world production, distribution and campaign buying. **Response:** compete only on a local, practical job Epic is not already solving for an Abuja event or small partner; avoid building an expensive UEFN-like editor, ad marketplace or payout pool.

### ZEPETO — avatar identity and brand activations

ZEPETO’s [brand-solutions page](https://brands.zepeto.me/en/solutions) sells virtual fashion, photo/video booths that generate user content, immersive brand worlds, ads including digital billboards, and online activations connected with offline sales. This is a direct benchmark for sponsor campaigns aimed at social/creator audiences, especially fashion and identity.

**Response:** design a small, measurable local activation rather than an avatar-fashion economy or a generic branded world; make any real-world redemption optional, partner-controlled and clearly measured.

### Maliyo Games — African storytelling and gamified event activation

Maliyo is a Nigerian game studio and production/talent benchmark, not an exact 3D Abuja-platform rival. Its 2025 [Africa Day updates](https://www.maliyo.com/maliyo-games-celebrates-africa-day-2025-with-app-store-spotlight-and-vibrant-game-updates/) used limited-time events, tournaments, daily missions, rewards and African-inspired design in titles including Safari City and Whot King. Safari City combines puzzle play with African neighbourhood renovation and story.

At [Moonshot 2025](https://www.maliyo.com/maliyo-games-at-moonshot-2025/), Maliyo ran a Safari City x Moonshot challenge with 25 limited-time match-3 levels, a leaderboard, special tokens and a smartphone prize, while also using a physical booth, live demos and a prize wheel. It bridged online participation and an in-person event rather than relying on a virtual world alone.

**Response:** culturally grounded missions and event tie-ins are proven creative tactics, not Vertualworld-owned territory. A local game studio or creator could be a production/distribution partner; the differentiation to test is an easy-to-repeat partner workflow and reliable measurement, not “we are the only African story.”

### Spatial — the cost of open-world hosting

Spatial said its Free and Pro creator tiers and their hosted 3D worlds would end on **27 July 2026** because the cost of hosting/scaling open multiplayer worlds could not be made sustainable at a price it considered acceptable. Its Enterprise service continued. The company also offered asset export before shutdown.

**Response:** do not make always-on multiplayer hosting the first product. Keep the world lightweight, preserve source assets and content portability, and add server costs only after a paying use case, concurrency and operating budget are known.

## 3. Substitutes for event and merchant budgets

### Tix Africa — event workflow, not just ticket checkout

Tix’s [Nigeria pricing page](https://tix.africa/pricing), checked on 7 October, says free events have no hosting fee and paid Nigerian tickets cost **8% + ₦100 per ticket**. It offers free, paid, invite-only, virtual, recurring and 1:1 event formats; ticket inventory; discount codes; sales dashboards; mobile check-in apps and check-in analytics; social sharing; attendee emails and reminders; discovery listings; and optional APIs, white-labelling, staffing and event equipment.

**Implication:** do not pitch Vertualworld as a ticketing replacement, take event payments or duplicate check-in. If the organiser uses Tix, link to its ticket page. A Vertualworld campaign should report only its own events—quest starts/completions, outbound link taps and voluntary partner-code/QR redemptions—and distinguish these from ticket purchases, attendance and sales.

### Google Business Profile and WhatsApp Business — free/familiar merchant tools

Google describes [Business Profile](https://business.google.com/en-all/business-profile/) as a free listing on Search and Maps. Its official page highlights reviews, products/services, offers and event posts, booking, ordering and reservation links. WhatsApp’s [Business App features](https://whatsappbusiness.com/products/business-app-features/) include a business profile, product catalogues and links, carts, QR/short-link entry points, quick replies, customer-management tools and promotions; availability and costs for some broadcast/ad features vary by market and account.

**Implication:** an additional paid business listing is weak. Vertualworld has to earn a budget by adding a compelling playable story or event activation and reporting a measurable incremental action. Use the business’s existing Google, WhatsApp, booking or order destination as the call to action. Never equate an impression, tap or QR scan with a sale.

### Selar — creator commerce already exists

Selar’s [official product pages](https://selar.com/features) position it as creator commerce for digital products, courses, memberships, services, physical goods and event tickets, with sales pages, affiliates and payment support. It is not a 3D-world competitor, but it is an existing way for Nigerian creators to package and sell work. If Vertualworld later supports creator monetisation, start by linking to a creator’s existing store rather than holding funds or building a payment marketplace prematurely.

## Strategic recommendations for Vertualworld

1. **Retain the Abuja-first market; drop “the 3D Abuja world” as the differentiator.** Public products already claim Abuja maps or life worlds, and the Lagos Life creator has announced Abuja expansion. Keep the current schematic scene as a low-cost demonstration stage; do not promise map accuracy or spend on a street-for-street rebuild without a buyer-backed reason and properly sourced/permissioned data.
2. **Position as an interactive campaign layer, not a city directory, ticketing platform or open-world MMO.** A candidate offer is one locally authored, partner-approved quest that moves from a shareable link/QR to the organiser’s existing ticket, booking or WhatsApp endpoint, with a small results report. This is a test—not a feature Vertualworld has already built.
3. **Test who pays.** Interview event organisers/venues about audience access and delivery; separately interview sponsors, brands and agencies about activation budgets and proof required. The organiser may be distribution/venue partner and the sponsor the payer. Do not assume either role until a buyer makes a concrete commitment.
4. **Measure actions, not attention claims.** Before a pilot, agree definitions for starts, completions, outbound clicks and optional redemptions. Report unique people only where measurement is valid and consented. Compare against the partner’s existing channel and distinguish scans/clicks from attendance, orders and revenue.
5. **Ride existing distribution.** Put the playable link in partner-owned WhatsApp, social, event and venue channels; send ticket purchases to the organiser’s existing provider. Do not require a new account or app install unless the experience genuinely needs one.
6. **Keep the product lean and safe.** Avoid open chat, real-money game balances, token promises, player-to-player transfers, a marketplace, paid ticketing, precise GPS and always-on servers during zero-budget validation. Existing `GC` remains fictional; no payment feature is being recommended or enabled by this research.
7. **Use local creators and studios without claiming exclusivity.** Test co-creation with Abuja storytellers, artists or game developers; assess whether Maliyo or event agencies could collaborate. Get permissions for names, images, logos, music and places.
8. **Treat name and product confusion as a watch item.** Search results show multiple contemporaneous products/pages using “Abuja Life/AbujaLife.” Keep Vertualworld’s brand and domain distinct, and do a formal name/trademark/domain review before investing in a public launch.

## Low-cost validation plan

### First 14 days: observe before coding

- Play or inspect the available Abuja Life entry points on a couple of ordinary phones; record what actually works, onboarding steps, load time, data use and whether the features advertised on their home page are available in the game. Do not create competitor accounts or submit real personal data unless necessary and appropriate.
- Ask 10 prospective players what they choose between the current Vertualworld demo and the Abuja/Lagos life-sim alternatives, and why. Ask about their last actual use, not hypothetical interest.
- Interview at least 6 Abuja venues/event operators, 3–5 event sponsors/brands/agencies, 3 organisers and 1–2 local creators. For each buyer, ask what they paid for the last activation, what evidence they trust, what tools they already use, and who controls the budget.
- Show Vertualworld on the prospect’s own phone. Ask for a budget-owner introduction, a written pilot scope, a date or permission—not just compliments.

### Days 15–30: run one bounded comparison

If a partner is willing, compare a short quest to that partner’s usual link/post for the same event or audience. Use only partner-approved content and the existing ticket/WhatsApp/booking destination. Agree the primary measure in advance; record starts, completions and outbound actions without claiming sales lift from traffic alone. Keep a simple 2D fallback if the current 3D demo is slow or difficult on the target phones. Do not build multiplayer, real-money payment or a self-service campaign editor for this test.

### Days 31–90: look for renewal, not a headline counter

Continue only if a buyer pays for a scoped pilot, commits credibly to a repeat, or gives a specific written date and budget path—and the delivery remains usable and supportable. Include labour, hosting, support, moderation and payment/legal costs when calculating contribution. If attention is high but no partner renews, test a different buyer problem or a simpler service before adding more world features.

## What to monitor monthly

- New Abuja or Nigeria life-sim launches; whether announced features actually ship; product ownership and brand/name clarity.
- Lagos Life’s verified unique and returning users (if later independently measured), time-on-site, outages, live city coverage, ad inventory, rate cards and documented campaign outcomes.
- Whether local sponsors renew, what they pay for, and whether they value a quest/activation more than a virtual billboard, social ad or event listing.
- Tix changes to pricing/integrations, and updates to WhatsApp or Google merchant tools.
- Roblox/Fortnite/ZEPETO changes in browser distribution, African reach, local creator eligibility, campaign reporting and brand safety.
- Actual phone performance, data use, repeat play and privacy/moderation workload in Vertualworld—not competitors’ headline counters alone.
